# Lesson 03 — Phones, codes, and QR (20 min)

**Audience:** all staff · **Materials:** examples [05](../examples/05-mfa-fatigue-otp.md), [06](../examples/06-qr-code-phishing.md), [07](../examples/07-sms-smishing.md)

## Objectives
1. Respond correctly to unexpected MFA prompts.
2. Never share one-time codes.
3. Treat QR codes and texts with the same care as links.

## Outline
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0–4 | Poll: "Have you ever received a prompt you did not start?" |
| 4–10 | Example 05: the late-night prompt storm. Rule: **Deny, don't share, report.** |
| 10–15 | Examples 06–07: QR and SMS. Preview the URL; use the official app. |
| 15–20 | Quick-fire: 5 mini scenarios, group says Deny / Report / Fine. |

## Talking points
- An unexpected prompt means someone has your password. Reset it.
- A code is like a key: nobody legitimate asks for it.
- A QR code is just a link you cannot read.
