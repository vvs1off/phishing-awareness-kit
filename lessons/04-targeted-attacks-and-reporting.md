# Lesson 04 — Targeted attacks and a reporting culture (30 min)

**Audience:** all staff, plus managers · **Materials:** examples [04](../examples/04-it-support-impersonation.md), [08](../examples/08-spear-phishing.md), [10](../examples/10-shared-document-lure.md), [reporting flow](../checklists/how-to-report.md)

## Objectives
1. Understand why tailored messages are harder to spot.
2. Verify unfamiliar contacts and shared files safely.
3. Report confidently and without fear.

## Outline
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0–5 | What public information about us could make a message convincing? |
| 5–15 | Example 08 and example 10: personalization and cloud-doc lures. |
| 15–20 | Example 04: why "do not tell colleagues" is the biggest red flag. |
| 20–27 | Walk the reporting flow; practice reporting a sample together. |
| 27–30 | Managers: how to respond when someone reports (thank them, never blame). |

## Talking points
- Good grammar and personal details do not mean safe.
- Verify through an independent channel.
- Reporting is a contribution, not a confession.
- A click is not a disaster; a hidden click is.
