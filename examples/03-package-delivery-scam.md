# Example 03 — Package-delivery scam: "Unable to deliver your parcel"

**Category:** Package-delivery scam · **Difficulty:** Beginner · **Channel:** Email

> **TRAINING SAMPLE — NOT A REAL MESSAGE.** Fictional carrier (SwiftPost Express). Links defused.

## The sample

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  EXAMPLE / TRAINING — NOT A REAL MESSAGE                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
From:     SwiftPost Express <noreply@swiftpost-track.example.org>          [1]
Subject:  Delivery attempt failed - action required (Ref 88213)

Hello customer,

We could not deliver your parcel because the address was incomplete.     [2]
Your package will be returned in 24 hours unless you pay a redelivery
fee of $1.99.                                                              [3]

    Reschedule delivery -> https://example.com/swiftpost-fee               [4]

Enter your card details on the next page to confirm.                       [5]
```

## Red flags

| # | Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Unfamiliar tracking domain, not the carrier's official site. | Check the domain, not the logo. |
| 2 | You may not be expecting any parcel; no order, item, or name is given. | Scams are generic by design. |
| 3 | **Tiny fee + deadline.** | Small amounts feel harmless; the real goal is your card data. |
| 4 | Link goes to a payment page off the carrier's site. | Real carriers rarely ask for card data via email links. |
| 5 | Asks for card details. | The fee is a pretext. |

## What to do instead

1. Do not click. Check the tracking number directly on the carrier's official website or app, which you open yourself.
2. Compare against orders you actually placed.
3. Report/delete. If you entered card data, call your bank to block the card.

## Discussion questions

- Why does a very small fee make this scam more effective?
- How can you check a parcel without using any link in the message?
