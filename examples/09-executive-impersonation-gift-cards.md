# Example 09 — Executive impersonation: "Quick favour, I'm in a meeting"

**Category:** BEC / executive impersonation · **Difficulty:** Beginner · **Channel:** Email or chat

> **TRAINING SAMPLE — NOT A REAL MESSAGE.** Fictional executive ("Alex Morgan, CEO") at Globex. No real person is represented.

## The sample

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  EXAMPLE / TRAINING — NOT A REAL MESSAGE                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
From:     Alex Morgan <alex.morgan.ceo@example.net>                        [1]
Subject:  Are you at your desk?

Are you available? I am in a meeting and cannot take calls. I need you
to handle something discreetly and quickly.                                [2]

Reply as soon as you see this.
Sent from my mobile                                                        [3]
```

*(Next message, after you reply: a request to buy gift cards and send the codes by photo.)* **[4]**

## Red flags

| # | Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Executive's name on a free or unrelated address. | Display names are trivial to fake. |
| 2 | Vague, secretive, urgent. | Opening lines are designed to get a reply first. |
| 3 | "Sent from my mobile" excuses odd style and no phone. | A cover for errors and for refusing verification. |
| 4 | Gift cards or unusual payments. | Gift-card codes are untraceable and cannot be recalled. |

## What to do instead

1. Do not reply. Contact the executive through a known channel.
2. Know that your organization has a rule: **no payments or purchases by email request alone**.
3. Report it to security. Managers: make it safe and easy to say "I verified and it was fake."

## Discussion questions

- Would you feel comfortable verifying a request from a senior leader? What would make that easier?
