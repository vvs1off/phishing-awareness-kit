# Example 08 — Spear-phishing: a tailored message

**Category:** Spear-phishing · **Difficulty:** Advanced · **Channel:** Email

> **TRAINING SAMPLE — NOT A REAL MESSAGE.** Fictional people and companies (Initech, "Priya Raman", "Marcus Hale"). Links defused.

## The sample

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  EXAMPLE / TRAINING — NOT A REAL MESSAGE                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
From:     Marcus Hale <marcus.hale@initech-partners.example.net>           [1]
To:       Priya Raman
Subject:  Following up from the Lisbon conference - slides + quick favour  [2]

Hi Priya,

Great meeting you at the supply-chain panel last week. I enjoyed your
point about vendor onboarding. Here are the slides I mentioned:

    https://example.com/shared/panel-slides                                [3]

One quick favour: could you review the attached contract draft before
our call and send over the signed copy by tonight?                          [4]
```

## Red flags

| # | Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Sender is **new to you** and from a look-alike or personal domain. | Attackers research targets from public profiles, so personal details prove nothing. |
| 2 | Specific details (event, topic) used to earn trust. | Spear-phishing works by feeling personal. |
| 3 | Link to a file-sharing page that may ask you to sign in. | A fake sign-in page steals credentials. |
| 4 | Asks for a **signed document by tonight**. | Combines an attachment, an action, and a deadline. |

## What to do instead

1. Verify the person through a channel you already trust (company directory, a known contact, the event organizer).
2. Open shared files only through your approved sharing tools, never through a password prompt in a message.
3. Slow down when a message is *very* well-tailored: that is itself a signal.
4. Report it. Your report helps protect colleagues who got the same message.

## Discussion questions

- What information about us is public, and could an attacker use it?
- How do we verify a new contact before sharing documents?
