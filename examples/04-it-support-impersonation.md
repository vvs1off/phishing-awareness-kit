# Example 04 — IT-support impersonation: "Install this security update"

**Category:** IT-support impersonation · **Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Channel:** Email + phone follow-up

> **TRAINING SAMPLE — NOT A REAL MESSAGE.** Fictional company (Initech). Links defused.

## The sample

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  EXAMPLE / TRAINING — NOT A REAL MESSAGE                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
From:     Initech Security Team <security-patch@initech-it.example.net>    [1]
Subject:  Critical: your laptop is infected - call us now

Our monitoring detected malware on your device (ID 7731-A).               [2]
To avoid data loss, call our technician at +1-555-0100 right away and
have remote access software ready so we can clean your computer.          [3]

Do not tell colleagues - the infection may spread through chat.           [4]

Download the cleaner: https://example.com/initech-cleaner.exe             [5]
```

## Red flags

| # | Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | External look-alike domain for an "internal" team. | Real IT mail comes from your organization's actual domain. |
| 2 | Alarming claim with no way to verify. | Fear is the hook. |
| 3 | **Asks for a phone call and remote access.** | Remote access gives an attacker full control. |
| 4 | **"Do not tell colleagues."** | Secrecy prevents the cross-check that would expose the scam. |
| 5 | `.exe` download from a link. | Executables from unsolicited messages are a major malware route. |

## What to do instead

1. Do not call the number or install anything.
2. Contact IT via the official helpdesk channel you already know.
3. Report the message. Tell your team: sharing is protective, not harmful.
4. If you already gave remote access: disconnect from the network, then call IT immediately.

## Discussion questions

- What does our real IT team **never** ask us to do?
- Where can everyone find the official helpdesk contact?
